# Corroborating interior tick readings with measured strokes

Fitted axis endpoints have fractional pixel coordinates. The original-image stroke scan now includes the pixel containing each endpoint. It checks rail continuity using samples centered within the fitted segment. A stroke still needs a continuous tip, an admissible width and depth, and two supporting rail samples. Horizontal and vertical axes use the same sampling convention in either direction.

An interior literal OCR reading can be checked against a scale established without any disputed crop. This check requires one lane of disjoint original-image crops with unique attached strokes, a valid inlier fit, and increasing primary values in axis order. Two undisputed crops establish the line and at least one other undisputed crop must agree. Each source must retain its original dominant decoder path. The primary must interpolate within the existing two-pixel fit tolerance. Every competing numeric literal must fall outside the uncertainty interval obtained by varying both anchor positions and the target stroke by that tolerance. The diagnostic retains the primary, competing readings, measured positions and anchor sources. The endpoint check remains separate.

Owned raster controls test both ends of the scan, reversed direction, detached ink, missing rail support and strokes beyond the endpoint pixel. A controlled workflow with supplied recognizer and marker outputs exported both known numeric rows after resolving an interior digit substitution. An alternative within the uncertainty interval, an independent recognizer, insufficient evidence and a primary inconsistent with the stroke still required Review.

On one nonsealed development graph the scanner found seven Y strokes, up from six. All six recognized Y labels had independent stroke associations, up from five. Three competing Y readings were corroborated. One printed Y label still lacked recognition. Y fit RMS was 0.211 pixels over six inliers, previously 0.199 over five. Two X labels were located between strokes and one had competing numeric readings. The automatic workflow continued to require calibration. Image-only runs took 3.92 and 4.58 seconds, respectively.

Both frozen runs retained the same 15 centers. Against 16 independent observations, one-to-one matching within five pixels gave 12 matches, three extra and four missing. Neither run emitted a numeric row. In a separate native WPF session, the unassisted export preview blocked export. Simulated review supplied three printed-axis anchors, moved one X coordinate, reassigned six series memberships, withheld one series, linked a shared baseline and explicitly accepted one point. Both phases remained unconfirmed. Save, reopen and export produced 12 rows with 12 matching sessions and four missing. Ten Y values were within one reference pixel and two were outside. Against the independent CSV, mean absolute Y error was 0.02395 graph units. These are exploratory development and assisted-workflow results.
